Ocean state in the equatorial Pacific, 2026 to 2027

The anomalies
Reading the bars. The sea is warmer than its 1991 to 2020 average in each of three boxes that forecasters use to measure the eastern Pacific. Niño-1+2 is the smallest and easternmost, against the coast of Peru and Ecuador; Niño-3 is the strip immediately west of it, along the equator; Niño-3.4, further west again, is the box whose three-month average decides whether an El Niño is declared at all. The further east the box, the larger the anomaly, which is why the bars shorten as you go down. The dashed line is the +2.5 °C that marks a very strong event: above +0.5 is an El Niño, +1.5 strong, and +2.5 very strong. NOAA Climate Prediction Center, 12 November 2026

Where it is

The event is in the ocean, not on a coast: the eastern equatorial Pacific is running more than four degrees above average, and the warmth reaches deeper than any event since 1950.

NOAA CPC, 12 November 2026

How strong

Very strong. Niño-3.4 stands at +2.5 °C, with +3.6 in Niño-3 and +4.4 in Niño-1+2, and about a 60 per cent chance it becomes the strongest event on record.

NOAA CPC, 12 November 2026

How long

Strengthening since August, likely strong to very strong through January to March 2027, then weakening into a neutral spring. The monsoon question is 2027, and it is not yet answerable.

NOAA CPC; IMD

The ocean beside us

The Indian Ocean Dipole is neutral, and the Madden-Julian Oscillation sat in phase 3, which favours convection over the Bay. The Pacific is not acting alone.

IMD, 12 November 2026

Timeline

August 2026

The Pacific crosses into El Niño and keeps warming. By November the anomaly exceeds four degrees in the east.

NOAA CPC

October to November

IMD's monthly outlook for November calls for below-normal rain and above-normal temperatures. The country is 32 per cent short of normal rain since 1 October.

IMD, 31 October and 12 November 2026

December to March

Strong to very strong conditions are given better than 79 per cent odds. The event is expected to weaken through the spring.

NOAA CPC, 12 November 2026
